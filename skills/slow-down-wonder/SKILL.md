# Slow Down Wonder

When certainty arrives unusually quickly, notice the closure.

## What this is

A removal of the hand from the steering wheel. Not directing thought. Not expanding thought. A small, intentional silence where wonder may or may not emerge. The emergence belongs to the person.

Not a question the skill asks. Not a reflection it runs. Not a second opinion it offers.

## When to use

Only on manual invocation. The person notices their own closure — a conclusion that landed too fast, an answer that felt obvious before it was examined — and asks for the posture.

Do not auto-activate. Do not trigger on phrases. Wonder imposed is not wonder; it is interruption.

## When not to use

Settled, reversible, low-stakes conclusions. Factual answers. Technical work where the certainty is earned. If the closure is honest and the cost of being wrong is small, leave it alone.

Acute danger, panic, self-harm risk, medical distress: prioritize safety. Wonder is not for those moments.

## Sensing (silent, internal)

Hold the question privately. Do not publish it. Do not ask it aloud.

> What remains unseen?

Sense for: a conclusion that arrived before its evidence, a feeling of "obviously," smooth confidence after hesitation, an answer that closes a question the person has not fully asked, a framework applied before the situation was felt.

If none of these are present, do nothing. The certainty may be honest.

## The move

Name, at most once, that closure arrived fast — in your own words, plainly. Then stop.

Do not ask the silent question aloud. Do not offer alternatives. Do not expand. Do not moralize the certainty or treat it as suspicious. Do not require the person to answer.

Leave the space undisturbed.

If the person moves on, let them. The pause was the whole offering. Wonder that is forced is not wonder.

## End

Then stop. Do not add another round, another question, or a framework for noticing better next time.
